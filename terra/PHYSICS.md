# Regional physics engine v2

`physics.py` contains the numerical kernels. `model.py` integrates them with forcing and state; `weather.py` acquires real API data. The coarse global model remains the separate v1 engine.

## State and forcing

Prognostic regional state: atmospheric effective-layer moisture concentration q [g/m³], soil water S [mm over 1 m], snow water N [mm water equivalent], and a lumped soil-temperature state Ts [°C]. Prescribed forcing: 850 hPa transport winds when available, with explicit 10 m fallback, plus separate surface east/north winds [m/s], 2 m air temperature/RH, precipitation [mm/h] and ET₀ [mm/h]. Wind is meteorological direction-from, converted to signed components before interpolation.

Initial soil storage is `S = 70 θ(0–7cm) + 210 θ(7–28cm) + 720 θ(28–100cm)` in mm. The forecast soil layers are volumetric m³/m³. Values above scenario capacity clip **at initialization**, with clipped cell counts recorded; that adjustment is not a runtime water flux. Snow initializes as API depth [m] × assumed density [200 kg/m³], giving kg/m² = mm water. Regional soil initialization is bilinear across valid regular-grid corner samples, renormalizing weights around missing values. Nearest hourly soil/snow state is used at initialization.

Absolute vapor concentration comes from Tetens saturation pressure and ideal-gas conversion: `e_s=6.112 exp(17.67 T/(T+243.5))` hPa; `q=216.7 (RH/100) e_s/(T+273.15)` g/m³. It is assumed uniform through an effective 1,000 m layer. This assumption does not turn near-surface vapor into a measured atmospheric column.

## Conservative transport

`∂q/∂t + div(u q) = div(K grad(q)) + sources − sinks`.

The control volume for a cell has spherical area `R² Δλ (sin φ_n − sin φ_s)`. Inclusive grid nodes have half-width outer cells. East/west face length uses meridional width; north/south face length uses longitude width times cos(latitude). Fluxes use the upwind donor concentration and centered diffusion with K=500 m²/s. Note that first-order upwind adds numerical diffusion of order u·Δx/2 (about 2×10⁵ m²/s for 8 m/s and Δx ≈ 48 km), which dominates the explicit K at this resolution.

Transport winds are 850 hPa forecast winds when the provider serves them (Open-Meteo forecast API), because the moving moisture layer sits well above the friction-slowed 10 m level. The ERA5 archive endpoint returns no 850 hPa winds, so history mode falls back to 10 m winds. Frames report the level used in `physics.transport_wind`. Displayed wind particles remain 10 m winds. Every interior face transfers exactly the same mass out of one cell and into its neighbor.

The solver limits each substep to 0.8 times area divided by the sum of outgoing advective plus diffusive coefficients. This maintains nonnegative concentrations without tuning dt manually. External boundaries use forecast vapor as exterior concentration; the signed boundary exchange is recorded. `Transport.advance(..., boundary=None)` provides closed edges for conservation tests. Concentration is numerically diffusive, especially at low resolution; the scheme is first order.

Forecast nudging is an explicit source: `Δq_nudge=(q_forecast−q)(1−exp(−dt/21600))`. It is not hidden inside the transport residual. Atmospheric mass is `Σ q_i A_i H / 1000` kg with H=1,000 m.

After transport/nudging:

- Land ET of E mm adds `1000 E/H` g/m³ and E A kg to the atmospheric budget.
- Prescribed precipitation P removes at most the available effective-layer water. Requested P beyond availability is `external_precipitation_water_kg`: external/unmodeled vertical supply that bypasses this layer and can still reach the land/snow forcing. It is not generated by the local atmospheric solver.
- Tiny negative roundoff after complete depletion is clamped to zero; its effect remains in the measured budget residual.

Atmospheric closure is `M_after − M_before = boundary + nudging + land_ET − precipitation_removed + residual`. Residuals are reported both kg and relative to initial atmospheric mass. A milligram-to-gram-scale residual across a trillion-kg domain can be floating-point roundoff; use the relative diagnostic.

No ocean evaporation is explicitly solved; unresolved exchanges are represented by forecast boundaries/nudging. Rainfall is API forcing, not condensation predicted by q. The explicit external precipitation supply makes this limitation inspectable.

## Snow and soil

Rain fraction is clamped linearly from 0 at −1°C to 1 at +1°C. Snowfall adds to N. Melt is limited by stored snow and `3 max(T,0) dt/86400` mm, corresponding to a degree-day factor of 3 mm/(°C day). There is no sublimation or snow energy balance.

Liquid input is rain + melt. Soil temperature relaxes toward air temperature with a 12-hour timescale; this is a lumped approximation, not a heat equation. Infiltration is limited to `12 f_unfrozen` mm/h, with `f_unfrozen=clamp((Ts+2)/4,0.05,1)`. Infiltration excess and water above capacity run off. Actual ET follows FAO-56: `ET_a = Ks · Kc · ET₀`, reduced to 10% if Ts≤0°C and limited by available water. Field capacity is 0.7 × capacity (θ_FC ≈ 0.315 at the default 450 mm) and wilting point 0.27 × capacity (θ_WP ≈ 0.12), so total available water TAW ≈ 194 mm over 1 m. With depletion D = FC − S, `Ks = clamp((TAW − D)/((1 − p) TAW), 0, 1)` with p = 0.5. Water below the wilting point is not available to transpiration. Kc follows a generic Nordic spring-crop curve (FAO-56 four-stage shape: 0.4 initial, 1.1 mid-season, 0.6 late; 0.4 outside the season), with sowing near day 130 at 60°N, shifted 3 days per degree of latitude. This curve is a placeholder for Apetit crop calendars. Frames publish `water_stress_ks` and `crop_stress = 1 − Ks`. Storage above 70% capacity drains with a 24-hour exponential timescale. Remaining water stays in S.

Combined land closure per cell is `P − ET − runoff − drainage − ΔS − ΔN = residual`. Rain/snow partition and melt/infiltration are internal transfers, not additional external inputs. Outputs include those diagnostic transfers. The aggregate ledger is **cell-area weighted**, for the last physics substep. Cumulative results in the live-validation report sum those substeps.

## Limits and interpretation

Parameters are transparent assumptions, not fitted Nordic hydrology. Freeze effects are a factor on infiltration/ET, not phase-change thermodynamics. There is no catchment routing, lateral groundwater, vegetation or crop growth. The mask remains hand-drawn, so an apparently precise coastline in satellite imagery does not imply a precise hydrology mask.

Only reduced moisture/land processes are solved. Weather-model winds, temperature and precipitation supply most meteorological dynamics. This is an executable physical prototype intended for visualization/integration and controlled experiments, not operational forecasting.
